# Sounds you can take

Sound effects made in code, rendered, ready to drop into an editor or a mixer. Every file here was computed by
[earshot](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/earshot)'s code: no recording, no sample library, nothing third party. How we use
them, with every level and every rule: [docs/19](../docs/19-sound-effects.md).

| folder | what | format |
|---|---|---|
| [`kit/`](kit) | **the kit we mix with**: 54 takes for 27 roles (keys, pops, a dry click, card thuds, whooshes, the message ding, a soft air swish for text, the hit...), exactly the files behind seven animated ads of November 2026. The role table, with what each one is for: [`kit/README.md`](kit/README.md) | WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit |
| [`bank/`](bank) | **the whole bank**: all 252 sounds earshot 0.4.0 ships, in 43 families, each rendered once by its id with no knobs (the delivered final). One line per sound with earshot's own description: [`bank/catalog.md`](bank/catalog.md); the same as data: [`bank/catalog.json`](bank/catalog.json) | MP3, 320 kbps CBR |

## How to use them

- **In an editor** (CapCut, Premiere, Resolve, Final Cut): drag the file onto a track. Set the level by ear or, better,
  by loudness against the rest of the mix: every file peaks at -1 dBFS as delivered, which says nothing about how
  loud it sounds. Two bank files were encoded 1 dB lower because their MP3 decode went over full scale
  (`pro_click/tink`, `toggle/on`; `mp3_gain_db` in the catalog).
- **Put the right point on the frame.** A click, a pop or a knock lands on its onset (the first few milliseconds); a
  whoosh lands on its **peak**, the loudest moment, which the catalog gives in seconds from the start of the file.
  Put that on the frame where the move is fastest.
- **In code**, render by id with earshot instead of decoding the MP3: you get the exact WAV, and the knobs (`--seed`
  for another take of the same sound, `--dur`, `--apex` to put a whoosh's peak where your move needs it, `--pitch` for
  the tonal families). The ids are the file paths: `bank/whoosh_fast/crisp.mp3` is
  `earshot sfx render whoosh_fast/crisp -o x.wav`.
- **The kit files** carry the knobs we chose (a 0.6 s whoosh with its peak at 0.25 s, the hit tuned to C2, the message
  ding at C6). `kit/kit.lock.json` gives each take's measured onset, peak and end.

What the build checked on every MP3: decoded with ffmpeg, it lines up with its WAV render to the sample (lag 0 by
cross-correlation, the encoder delay is in the file's LAME header), it is at most 64 samples longer (padding at the
end), and its decoded peak stays under -0.3 dBFS. A player that ignores the LAME header adds about 23 ms of silence at
the start; for sample-exact timing, render the WAV by id.

## What they are, honestly

- **Made by code.** 237 sounds are recipes (noise, modal resonators, filters, a computed room). 15, the `fit/`
  family, are recipes fitted from CC0 recordings: the structure of the sound (modes, partials, envelopes) was
  measured from a recording and the samples are computed; no recording is here. Credits at the end of
  [`bank/catalog.md`](bank/catalog.md).
- **Chosen by measurement, not by a listening test.** earshot's author says so plainly. Joaquín approved the kit by
  ear on our films; the rest of the bank nobody here has judged.
- **They read as code-made** to earshot's realness model (our films scored 0.47 to 0.55 overall, the densest stretch
  0.14). The `fit/` sounds read the most real (a click 0.81, a switch 0.89, a bamboo tok 0.92): reach for them first.
- **Some do not sound like their name.** Measured on our films: `swipe/paper`, `cloth` and `velvet` read as an
  impact hit, never use them as whooshes; `typing_key/deep` is a 71 Hz thump; `pro_zip/up` reads as a record scratch.
  Joaquín ruled out the `shimmer`, `pro_shimmer`, `logo_sting` and `pro_logo_sting` families (the "fairy" sparkle) and
  any sequence of pitched notes. [docs/19](../docs/19-sound-effects.md) has the rest.
